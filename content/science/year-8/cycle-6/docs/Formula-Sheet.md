# Formula sheet and worked examples

Use SI units unless a ratio uses the same unit in numerator and denominator. Use g = 9.8 N/kg in this pack. A school assessment may specify another value.

| Quantity | Formula | Worked example |
|---|---|---|
| Kinetic energy | Eₖ = ½mv² | ½ × 0.8 kg × (3 m/s)² = 3.6 J |
| Gravitational potential energy | Eᵍ = mgh | 1.5 × 9.8 × 2 = 29.4 J |
| Current | I = V/R | 9/30 = 0.3 A |
| Voltage | V = IR | 0.4 × 15 = 6 V |
| Resistance | R = V/I | 6/0.02 = 300 Ω |
| Efficiency | useful/input × 100% | 90/150 × 100 = 60% |
| Other output energy | input − useful, if no energy remains stored | 150 − 90 = 60 J |
| Rebound percentage | rebound/drop × 100% | 78 cm/120 cm × 100 = 65% |
| Series voltage | supply = sum of component drops | 12 V − 7 V = 5 V remaining |
| Parallel supply current | sum of branch currents | 0.12 + 0.18 = 0.30 A |
| Mean (added practice) | sum of results/number of results | (66 + 70 + 68)/3 = 68 cm |
| Range (added practice) | maximum − minimum | 70 − 66 = 4 cm |

Conversions: 1 kg = 1000 g; 1 m = 100 cm; 1 kJ = 1000 J. Convert before substituting into energy formulas. Keep full working values and round only the final answer.

Common traps: forgetting v²; dividing by 2 twice; mixing cm and m; reading the wrong graph category; using I/V for resistance; reversing an efficiency fraction; forgetting energy left stored; dividing voltage across parallel branches; assuming a percentage tells you total energy without knowing input.

Sources: S Formulas, Energy Efficiency and Types of circuits; C Q11–16/Q19. Mean/range are explicitly added practice. Rebound interpretation requires the conditions explained in the study guide.
