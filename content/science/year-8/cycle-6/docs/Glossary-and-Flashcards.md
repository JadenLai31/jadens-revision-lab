# Glossary and flashcards

Original explanations aligned to the supplied materials; investigation terminology is added practice.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Energy | Capacity to do work or cause change. |
| Joule | SI unit of energy, symbol J. |
| Kilojoule | 1000 joules, symbol kJ. |
| Work | Energy transferred mechanically when a force acts through a displacement. |
| Kinetic energy | Energy an object has because it moves relative to the chosen frame. |
| Mass | Amount of matter, measured in kilograms in these formulas. |
| Speed | Distance travelled per unit time; use m/s in the kinetic-energy formula. |
| Potential energy | Energy stored because of position or condition. |
| Gravitational potential energy | Energy associated with position in a gravitational field. |
| Elastic potential energy | Energy stored by elastic deformation such as stretching or compression. |
| Extension | Increase in length compared with the unstretched length. |
| Chemical energy | Energy associated with chemical substances, including food and battery materials. |
| Nuclear energy | Energy associated with atomic nuclei. |
| Thermal energy | Energy associated with microscopic particle motion and interactions. |
| Sound | Energy transfer through vibrations in a medium. |
| Light | Energy transfer by electromagnetic waves. |
| Energy transfer | Energy moving from one object or place to another. |
| Energy transformation | A change in energy form. |
| Conservation of energy | Total energy cannot be created or destroyed. |
| System | The objects or region chosen for an energy analysis. |
| Useful energy | Energy output in the form intended for the task. |
| Wasted energy | Energy transferred in forms less useful for the intended task; it is not destroyed. |
| Efficiency | Useful output energy divided by total input energy, usually expressed as a percentage. |
| Rebound percentage | Rebound height divided by release height times 100, with consistent height definitions. |
| Circuit | An electrical arrangement that can provide conducting paths; an open circuit has an interrupted path. |
| Load | Component that transfers electrical energy into another form. |
| Conductor | Material through which electric charge can move readily. |
| Insulator | Material that strongly resists charge movement. |
| Short circuit | Unintended low-resistance bypass of part of a circuit. |
| Electron | Negatively charged particle. |
| Current | Rate of flow of electric charge. |
| Ampere | Unit of current; one coulomb per second. |
| Voltage | Energy transferred per unit charge between two points. |
| Volt | Unit of voltage; one joule per coulomb. |
| Resistance | Opposition to current, measured in ohms. |
| Ammeter | Current-measuring instrument connected in series. |
| Voltmeter | Voltage-measuring instrument connected across two points in parallel. |
| Conventional current | Direction defined for positive charge flow; opposite electron drift in metal wires. |
| Ohm’s law | For an ohmic conductor at constant conditions, V = IR with constant R. |
| Series | Components arranged along a single current path. |
| Parallel | Branches connected across the same two points. |
| DC | Direct current, flowing in one direction. |
| AC | Alternating current, periodically reversing direction. |
| Frequency | Number of complete cycles per second, measured in hertz. |
| Independent variable | Factor deliberately changed in an investigation. |
| Dependent variable | Outcome measured in an investigation. |
| Controlled variable | Factor kept consistent to support a fair comparison. |
| Repeatability | Consistency of results under repeated use of the same method and conditions. |
| Validity | How well an investigation supports the question and intended inference. |
| Systematic error | A consistent bias or offset not removed just by repeating measurements. |
| Prototype | A preliminary design built for testing and improvement. |

## Reasoning prompts

**Why does doubled speed quadruple kinetic energy?**

Speed is squared in E_k = ½mv²; (2v)² = 4v².

**What must a complete transfer explanation name?**

The energy form, starting object/place and receiving object/place.

**Why does a lower bounce not mean energy was destroyed?**

Less mechanical energy is recovered because energy has been transferred to other forms and surroundings.

**What condition belongs with “more resistance gives less current”?**

Voltage must be constant; I = V/R.

**Can parallel branches have different currents?**

Yes. Their voltages are equal but different resistances give different currents.

**What does repeating trials fail to fix?**

A systematic bias such as an incorrect ruler zero.

**What are four stages of improving a prototype?**

Develop, test against criteria, evaluate and improve, then retest.

**Does a lamp consume charge?**

No. It transfers electrical energy while charge continues through the circuit.

**What should you check before reading a graph value?**

The requested category/time, axis labels, scale and units.
