Shogun terminology

Oxford / class reading map · 16.4–16.6 and 16.8

An emperor could confer legitimacy while the shogun exercised practical military and governing power through the bakufu. A daimyō governed a domain, a samurai belonged to the warrior class, an ashigaru was a foot soldier and a rōnin lacked a lord. Bushido, the way of the warrior, groups ideals such as loyalty, honour and discipline that were interpreted differently over time. Practise Japanese readings separately from the meanings.

Recall and explain · Explain the difference between a political title, a government and a military role.

Shogunate Japan hierarchy

Oxford / class reading map · 16.5–16.6

The emperor had high formal standing and the shogun generally headed the military government. Daimyō relied on retainers for service and households for labour and revenue. The classroom model ranks warriors, farmers, artisans and merchants. Farmers were valued for producing food. Merchants could be wealthy despite their lower official position. Some marginalised groups sat outside the four-status model. In both Japan and medieval Europe, lords relied on warriors and agricultural production. The political arrangements differed across regions and periods.

Recall and explain · Explain formal status, practical power and wealth using three different examples.

Battle of Dan-no-ura

Oxford / class reading map · 16.1 and 16.4 for context; class reading for the battle

In 1185 the Minamoto defeated the Taira (Heike) in a naval battle in the Shimonoseki Strait. Yoshitsune led Minamoto forces at the battle. Emperor Antoku died during the Taira defeat. Yoritomo, a different Minamoto leader, was appointed shogun in 1192. The class retelling credits the tide and Taguchi Shigeyoshi’s change of sides. Use attribution for these details. Its Heike crab story is folklore. The Tale of the Heike is a literary tradition that can reveal how the conflict was remembered.

Recall and explain · Separate an event, a claim about its cause and a legend about its memory.

Weapons and artefacts

Oxford / class reading map · 16.8 for samurai context; class weapons list

Katana and tachi are curved swords distinguished in part by conventional mounting. The katana was usually worn edge-up and the tachi suspended edge-down. A tantō is a short blade. A naginata is a polearm associated in part with some warrior women. Shuriken came in varied forms. When studying an artefact, record its date, materials, provenance and what the object can establish about the society that produced it.

Recall and explain · Identify the five class artefacts and explain what a single museum object can tell us.

Women in Shogunate Japan

Oxford / class reading map · 16.7 and the class Tomoe Gozen handout

Class, period and household shaped women’s experiences. Elite women could manage households, study arts or enter marriages arranged to support alliances. Farming women contributed to agricultural labour, and merchant or artisan women could help in family businesses. Inheritance practices varied. Some warrior women received martial training. Tomoe Gozen’s portrayal challenges gender expectations, while dramatic details need corroboration. Geisha were trained entertainers, and tea ceremony was a distinct cultural practice.

Recall and explain · Compare two households and qualify any general claim about women.

End of Shogunate Japan

Oxford / class reading map · 16.10 and 16.13

Use the class mnemonic Six Big Battles Shaped Brave Meiji: Sakoku, Bakumatsu/Osaka Rebellion, Black Ships, Sonnō Jōi, Boshin War and Meiji Restoration. For actual chronology, put the Osaka Rebellion (1837) before Perry (1853). Sakoku restricted foreign contact through regulated channels. The Convention of Kanagawa (1854) gave limited port access. Kaikoku means opening the country. Yoshinobu returned governing authority in 1867. The Restoration began in 1868 and the Boshin War ran from 1868 to 1869. Fukoku kyōhei captures a goal of modernising industry and strengthening the military.

Recall and explain · Explain how existing domestic tensions and foreign pressure interacted.

Modern-day Japan

Oxford / class reading map · School geography task

Japan is an archipelago. On an atlas outline, locate Hokkaidō, Honshū, Shikoku and Kyūshū, then Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, Hiroshima and Mount Fuji. Include BOLTSS: border, orientation, legend, title, scale and source. Compare the plate names on your class map with the labels on the source you use. Plate movement contributes to earthquake risk, and some offshore earthquakes displace enough water to cause tsunamis. Demographics describes population characteristics. For a modern issue, explain who is affected, suggest a response and identify evidence of its effect.

Worked source analysis

Prompt. How useful is the Heike crab legend to a historian?

Model. It helps explain how people remembered the Taira defeat, because the legend connects local crabs to fallen warriors. Its folklore form limits its value for verifying what happened during the battle. A historian could compare written accounts and check when each was produced.

Start with the question the source can answer. Use a specific detail, explain what the detail shows, then name one limit.
